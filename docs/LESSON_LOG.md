- Data: issue 7 on the iPhone: 72% first try, merit, 20.9 minutes (8.7 of them drills), "Too hard" and fun "Boring", story "OK" (9 seconds on the opening); hardest gần, phải, bớt, đắt. - The learner wrote: "I need to understand better prices, like when to use lam or rau or nam for example" ("rau" is almost certainly rưỡi) and "Would be nice if the article was toed in better with the content of the lesson."
- So this issue is a numbers clinic, short and gentle: four new words, each one spoken number form with one rule, five review words heard but not typed, no warm-up, 33 answers before rush hour (41 in issue 7), at most one tricky option per question, and every tricky option tests the rule just taught.
- Words (4, one group): nhăm (five after the tens, the Hà Nội form of lăm), mốt (one after the tens from 20), tư (four after the tens from 20), chục (a ten, as sellers count). Contrasts avoid hỏi against huyền: nhắm, một (the rule's own pair), từ, chúc. Hooks: tasting an orange slice ("yum" with ny), the knight of một climbing back out of the moat, a grinning group photo (ư with spread lips, the old Chinese four of the Four Immortals), a tray of ten eggs set down with a thud ("chook", rhymes with book; no hen, so it does not cue gà). No did-you-know facts on the cards: the beats carry them.
- Glosses: nhăm and lăm mean the same, and chục and mươi overlap, which PEDAGOGY.md normally forbids teaching together. Here the contrast is the point, so the glosses are written to be told apart: nhăm "the Hà Nội way to say lăm (five after the tens)" against lăm's "five, after the tens (as in 15 and 25)"; chục "a ten, a set of ten (as sellers count)" against mươi's "-ty, the tens (as in 20)". Listening options are drawn at random from all nine words, so the pairs can meet.
- Review (5, heard, not typed): lăm, rưỡi, năm, mươi, trăm, the words the learner's question is about. gần, bớt, đắt and phải are not reviewed here (the review desk has them); bớt and đắt appear once, in the pomelo reaction, and đắt in the dialogue.
- The rules, in one grammar note: five is năm at the start, lăm or (in Hà Nội often) nhăm after mười or mươi, and năm again after linh (105); one is mốt after the tens from 20, but 11 is mười một; four is usually tư after the tens from 20, but 14 is mười bốn; ten is mười for 10 to 19, mươi for the tens from 20, chục for a ten when counting or pricing; rưỡi adds half of the unit before it (trăm rưỡi 150, hai nghìn rưỡi 2.500).
