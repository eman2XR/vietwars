# Lesson log

Why each lesson was planned the way it was, newest first. Written by the `/next-lesson` skill.

## 2026-11-22: Issue 4, People and family ("A country of Nguyễns")

- Data: issue 3 on the Mac: 81% first try, merit, 17.6 minutes, "Just right", fun "Good", story "Skipped it" (14 seconds on the opening; the two word beats read for 8 and 1 seconds). Hardest: hai, ba, trăm, nghìn, nữa. Wants next: "surprise me". Note: "the did you know popups I mostly skipped. need more illustrations". Real life 5 of 8, and 5 of the 17.6 minutes: a fare of sáu mươi nghìn typed 231000 (4 plays and a hint), the bill of 105,000 typed 10000 (5 plays and a hint), the tourist price for oranges answered Ngon quá!. Typing on lenient grades: wrong words for nghìn and trăm (both typed trien), tám (tram), bốn (bơ) and nữa (nhua). Listening: hai heard as five twice. The short review afterwards: mươi typed làm, lăm heard as the tens, rưỡi typed driou, triệu right.
- Theme: "surprise me", so the next planned unit, people and family, taught as the words you call everyone by. Vietnamese has no all-purpose you: the waiter is em, the bánh mì seller chị or cô, the xe ôm driver chú. It is useful from the first order in Hà Nội (every request starts with X ơi!), it surprises an English speaker, and it builds on food, numbers and prices through the real-life lines and combos.
- Story: "A country of Nguyễns" (en.wikipedia Nguyen, Vietnamese name, Pronouns in Vietnamese; vi.wikipedia Họ người Việt Nam, Đại từ nhân xưng): about a third of Vietnamese are Nguyễn (estimates run from 30 to 39 percent); the name people use comes last and goes with a family word (anh Dũng, ông Dũng); no all-purpose you; why so many (the Lý clan renamed in 1232, Hồ and Mạc families after 1407 and 1592); ông trẻ and "Bé bằng củ khoai, cứ vai mà gọi"; the scholar exiled in 1841 for using the royal double name Nguyễn Phúc; Nguyen in Australia and Iceland. Six short beats (under 240 characters each, against 600 before), each with a picture, landing after 2, 3, 18, 20, 21 and 49 answers.
- Words (10, in two parts): tên, tôi, anh, chị, em, then cô, chú, ông, bà, bạn. Ten rather than nine because with nine the engine has no free slot: a second tied beat lands on the same screen as the next untied one. Left for later: bác (its gloss overlaps cô and chú), con and cháu (moved to the politeness unit), vợ, chồng, gia đình, người, bố, mẹ. Contrasts avoid hỏi against huyền: tối, ảnh, chỉ, êm, cố, chủ, ống, ba (three, the learner's own ba and bà confusion), bán, bàn.
- Review (4): trăm, nghìn, mươi and nữa, the recall failures on lenient grades and the number words behind the missed fare and bill; nữa is also at 0.33 for meaning and was marked hardest. hai and ba, also marked hardest, are fine on lenient grades (hai typed perfectly, ba a tone slip), so they are heard in use instead: ba cà phê, hai trăm nghìn, hai mươi nghìn. lăm, triệu and rưỡi wait for the review desk or the time issue.
- Real life (7, every one with a picture): round numbers by ear (a fare of bảy mươi nghìn and a bill of hai trăm nghìn typed in digits; three coffees at một trăm năm mươi nghìn against 105.000, 15.000 and 50.000), a tourist price (trà đá at năm mươi nghìn, ten times năm nghìn), and three moments of choosing the family word (Cô ơi! to a bánh mì seller your mother's age, Chú ơi, bao nhiêu tiền? to a xe ôm driver your father's age, Tên tôi là Nam. to Bạn tên là gì?).
- Fewer facts: did-you-know facts on 4 of the 10 words, only the surprising ones (tôi first meant servant; women keep their family name; Anh yêu em; Teachers' Day on 20 November, two days before this issue). The checker no longer warns about words without a fact.
- Pictures: 11 new icons in `web/js/pictures.js`. anh, chị, em and bạn are two standing figures: the tinted one is the word, the plain one is you, and height shows who is older. cô has short permed hair and an áo dài collar, chú a pith helmet and moustache, ông glasses and a long beard, bà white hair in a bun; tôi puts a hand on the chest; tên is a name badge; the first beat has a full-name card with Dũng boxed. A new cover, `genealogy`: a gia phả scroll whose family tree is all NGUYỄN except the wife, TRẦN (an improvised seal glyph looked like the character 工 and became an abstract lotus). Checked large and in all seven styles with `scripts/picture_sheet.py`.
- Combos (5): Tên tôi là Nam., Chị ơi, bao nhiêu tiền?, Em ơi, cho anh một cốc nữa., and the trap pair hai mươi nghìn and hai trăm nghìn. Grammar: family words are I and you. 58 answers before rush hour after the reviews (issue 3 had 60).
- Curriculum: numbers-basics done, people active; market moved up after politeness, since prices by ear are still the weakest real-life skill.
- Fact-check review: the 31.5 percent figure has no reliable source (the wikis even disagree on the order of the next names), so the intro gives the sourced range; the scholar was renamed in 1842, not 1841, and his offence was the royal double surname Nguyễn Phúc, which Minh Mạng had banned, not the emperor's own name; Australia's ranking was 20 years old and contradicted elsewhere, replaced by the 2010 US census (38th); Iceland is right, but "named after their father" became "a father's first name plus -son or -dóttir"; "never Mr Nguyễn" became "almost never" (Bác Hồ); cô and chú are for people a bit younger than your parents, bác for older.
- Vietnamese review: tôi is neutral, safe with strangers but cold with friends and family (not "polite with anyone"), in the gloss, note and grammar; the cô tip said "soft g", which in English is the j of "gem"; the ông hook said "om", which many readers say like Tom (om, to braise), now a quick "ohm", with ôm (to hug, as in xe ôm) as the contrast; the em hook said "call out", the sắc cue, for a level word; a low five for bạn, not a shouted high five; ạ in the challenge's question; bạn is not a family word.
- Learning design review: the 105,000 bill the learner typed as 10000 was never heard, so the three coffees now cost một trăm linh năm nghìn, with 10.000₫ among the options; the tourist price now says what locals pay; part 2's first group no longer ends on two grandparents (bà and bạn swapped), and bà's hook (a chair, like ông's) is now market baskets; the beats were reordered so the story runs from names and family words to rank, the renamings, the banned double name and the emigrants (landing at 2, 3, 20, 21 and 51 answers, the last on the results page); the pull quote was dropped because it repeated the last beat and could pop up early as a fact; the xe ôm driver no longer calls you anh, which seemed to break the partner rule; distractors now differ by age (Bà ơi!, Ông ơi), and the name question offers real confusions; combos teach hết before fares and bills, and add mười lăm nghìn and năm mươi nghìn (lăm was heard as the tens in the last review); issue 2's mươi hook still had the Fat Cat from the battle version, and trăm, nghìn and nữa had no notes for reviews; two new beat pictures, a royal edict for 1232 and a suitcase for the emigrants.
- Not yet done: the three reviews and the site test (`check_site.py`); the owner runs them before publishing.

## 2026-11-21: Ask the editors, inked pictures, cleaner updates

- Ask: the learner wanted to ask quick questions. The Mac's on-device model was tested and answered Vietnamese wrongly, so questions are posted as GitHub issues (the ? button in an issue, or Ask on the home page) and the lesson loop answers each one as a comment with phrases to play (`scripts/questions.py`, checked by a second agent first). Questions and answers go into `learner/questions.json` and the summary, so later issues can teach what was asked.
- Pictures: at the learner's request every picture was redrawn in a hand-drawn editorial style (a colour field per subject, a paper cut-out, dip-pen ink with a slight tremor), using the site's own colours so all six styles and dark mode still work. The first covers looked crude at full size on the Mac; they were refined with finer lines, layered depth, hatching and woodblock-style waves. Issue 3's feedback also asked for more illustrations and said the did-you-know pop-ups were mostly skipped: plan issue 4 with pictures for more of its moments and fewer pop-ups.
- Updates: a Mac showed black pictures because Safari mixed a cached old file with a new stylesheet. The Pages build now stamps the service worker with the commit, the worker always revalidates app files, open pages reload once onto a new version, a page left open for six hours reloads on return, and a lesson file that fails to load is retried.

## 2026-11-21: Issue 3, One to ten, properly ("The bride price of nines")

- Data: issue 2 finished on the iPhone in the old battle version: 64% first try, 30 minutes, "Too hard", fun "OK"; lost the battle, knocked out six times. Listening was strong (meaning 100% for most words, rush hour 11 of 11); typing 13% (letters without marks: tien, dong, dat; and wrong words: tram for nghìn, hon for nữa); tone-spelling picks (trăm to trắm, lăm to lắm, mươi to mười). Meaning mix-ups among similar words: trăm, nghìn and triệu; nữa and rưỡi; đắt and cheap. The warm-up missed ba, bốn, tám and chín.
- The learner's note: "I need more and better hints, more and better memory hooks. I need to also learn 1-10 properly. Too much focus on the tones. Need more real life use of these words as well. Ie waiter says a price and i have to translate." Also: no quizzes about the article; a short intro with the story sprinkled through the lesson; save progress without finishing; track answer times, failed attempts and struggle data.
- Engine changes: lenient typing by default (right letters count; marks shown; strict in Settings); at most three spelling questions per part; three-level hints (hook, consonants, answer); wrong typed words come back once as syllable tiles; a Real life round (situations: hear a price, pick it or type digits); story beats tied to words, shown right after the word; no quiz; save and resume with unfinished results sent; issues over eight words in two parts; per-answer replays, time to first key, deletions and revealed answers; per-word struggle score in the learner summary.
- Issue 3: the numbers one to ten as real words with new hooks and did-you-know facts (Hai Bà Trưng, the folk verse of three trees, the Four Immortals, numbered weekdays, tái and chín in a phở shop, marks out of ten), eight real-life lines (chicken phở, two coffees, bánh mì, two bowls, a taxi, a tourist price for oranges to answer with Đắt quá!, a waiter's 95,000, a bill of 105,000), counting with bát and cốc, and a review of five issue 2 words: trăm, nghìn, đắt, nữa and gà. Story: Sơn Tinh and Thủy Tinh, the bride price of nines, in five beats: the suitors after hai, the nine-fold gifts after chín, the wedding on the second practice screen, the floods on the real-life screen and the Hùng Kings' day on the results page. 60 answers before rush hour (issue 2 had 78).
- Fact-check review: Ba Vì is on Hà Nội's western edge (part of the city since 2008); the bride price included a hundred trays of sticky rice, a hundred baskets of bánh chưng, and a pair of each nine-fold animal; Thủy Tinh was trying to carry her off, not win her back; added the saying "Dù ai đi ngược về xuôi, nhớ ngày giỗ Tổ mùng mười tháng ba"; 8 March is on the ordinary calendar; Wednesday is thứ tư. Issue 2's Đồng Xuân market is a different đồng, not copper.
- Learning-design review: the story played out of order (the wedding before the suitors), because a beat tied to a word followed its group in word order. The engine now always plays beats in file order, and `scripts/plan.mjs` shows where each lands (check_lessons warns on bunching and on more than 60 answers). The digits questions showed the answer as their placeholder (removed; the checker now catches it). Review cut from eight words to five: all review lands in the last part, and the issue 2 typing figure of 13% was strict grading; leniently it was 71%, and the real misses were recall (nghìn typed tram twice, nữa typed hon). triệu, rưỡi and bao nhiêu (meaning 100%) wait for the review desk or a time-telling issue. Real life now uses gà, đắt, trăm and nữa, the waiter's options sound like the answer (95, 90, 19, 59), and two bowls cost tám mươi nghìn so tám is heard. New hooks for một, ba, bốn, năm and mười (the dog for bốn and the sow for sáu were both animals with rising voices, and the learner mixed up bốn and sáu). The floods beat now says, from Lĩnh Nam chích quái, that they come every year around the seventh and eighth lunar months. Real life gained a hint (the line split at nghìn), "25k" counts as 25,000, and reply options are played after the answer.
- Second Vietnamese review (of the changed hooks, lines and combo): nữa means more or another, and the "one" in one more comes from một; the combo is now "Cho mình một cốc trà đá nữa." (tôi sounds stiff at a tea stall, and cốc follows the grammar note); the một tip says the t is not released, so it is not read as mộ; the bốn and mười hooks no longer contain the English word "one"; the bốn tip says rounded, since a Southern British "bone" starts unrounded. The real-life lines, prices and tháng bảy, tháng tám were confirmed.

## 2026-11-21: Redesign as a culture magazine (issues 1 and 2 rewritten)

- What the learner said: the battles with cartoon villains "looked like something made for a child"; they want "interesting things about Vietnamese history and culture" with images, it "should still be fun", and "cartoons are fine, just in context, not random bugs or cat". They chose the "culture magazine, no battles" direction.
- Changes: every lesson is now an issue with a sourced feature article, a trivia quiz, a did-you-know fact on each word, fact cards on three-in-a-row streaks, a rush hour round (Giờ cao điểm) in place of the boss fight, a stamp for a passport, and a street challenge. Villains, hearts, XP and ranks are gone. Covers are original scenes in context (a phở stall; Đồng Xuân market with a street vendor). Six styles named after Vietnamese materials replace the eight game styles.
- Issue 1, "Younger than the Eiffel Tower": phở (1900 to 1907, Nam Định), bánh mì, coffee (thought to be 1857), egg coffee, chicken phở (1939), pilotless phở in the rationing years. Issue 2, "Copper, paper, plastic": đồng as copper, coins reintroduced in 2003 and dropped in 2011, polymer notes, Đồng Xuân market (1889, the year of the Eiffel Tower), Thăng Long (1010) and the name Hà Nội (1831).
- Fact-check review (against English and Vietnamese Wikipedia): corrected the coin history (coins exist but have not been issued since 2011), the polymer rollout (2003 to 2006), the 500,000 note's colour, the market's rebuilding (partly, after the 1994 fire), egg coffee (fresh milk was scarce), the 2025 province merger (Nam Định is now part of Ninh Bình), rau sống (not served with Hà Nội phở), "Ăn cơm chưa?" (a mealtime greeting among family and neighbours), and the lesson 1 intro count. Added tiền mừng tuổi against the Southern lì xì, two market proverbs, and Đông Kinh as the source of "Tonkin".
- Images: Wikimedia Commons is blocked from the sandbox; the owner was asked to allow upload.wikimedia.org and commons.wikimedia.org. Until then, illustrations are original SVG.

## 2026-11-21: Lesson 2, Big numbers and prices (Vua Muỗi, the Mosquito King)

- Data: one session (lesson 1 on a Mac, 76% first try, 9.7 minutes, speed round 14/15). Feedback: "Too easy", fun "OK", hardest gà, bò, cá; note "make it more fun and unexpected (hint: the website name is Viet Wars)". In conversation: knows all lesson 1 words except sometimes gà, cá and bò; knows phở, trà and cơm well; no Vietnamese keyboard on the Mac; wants more pictures.
- Reading the data: the low scores for phở, trà and cơm came from spelling questions and typing without marks (pho, ca phe, nuoc, and "cha" for trà, which is how trà sounds in Hà Nội), not from meaning. A pitch track showed the Linh voice says hỏi as a plain fall like huyền, which explains phở picked as phờ. gà and bò were missed on meaning ("ca" for chicken, "co" for beef).
- Engine changes made for this lesson: battles with villains, hearts, critical hits, ambushes, power-ups and a boss round (the "Viet Wars" hint); on-screen tone and vowel keys; partial credit for Hà Nội sound-alike spellings; meaning and spelling tracked separately; a site-wide known list; "I know it" now skips a word's drills; warm-ups for probably-known basics; a picture for every word; eight visual styles.
- Known list: the nine lesson 1 words other than gà, cá and bò. Review: gà, cá and bò, typed from the picture (they were missed on meaning), with new notes and distinct hooks in lesson 1 (bò "b as in beef", cá "c as in carp"; the two bird images were part of the mix-up).
- Theme: numbers were next on the roadmap, and prices make them useful at once. Numbers 0 to 10 are a warm-up only. Fourteen new words because lesson 1 was too easy: trăm, nghìn, triệu, mươi, lăm, linh, rưỡi, tiền, đồng, bao nhiêu, đắt, rẻ, hơn, nữa. They bring in all six tones, including the nặng and ngã that lesson 1 lacked.
- Contrasts avoid hỏi against huyền (see above): trăm/trắm, triệu/triều, mươi/mười, lăm/lắm, linh/lính, tiền/tiến/tiện, đồng/đông/động, đắt/đất/đặt, rẻ/rẽ (hỏi and ngã), hơn/hôn, nữa/nửa.
- Reviews. Vietnamese accuracy: 105 is một trăm linh năm in Hà Nội (lẻ is Southern); I had it backwards. Hook fixes so they do not teach wrong sounds (nghìn started with "grin", chục implied the vowel of "but", tiền lost its iê glide, bao nhiêu's question rise pushed toward a rising tone); "muỗi đốt" instead of "muỗi cắn". Learning design: added the tens (mươi) and lăm, without which most prices cannot be said; dropped mấy (clashes with bao nhiêu), nửa (one mark from nữa, and a near-synonym of rưỡi) and chục (a near-synonym of mươi); combos became real prices plus the trap hai mươi năm (twenty years); the Mosquito King became a rip-off merchant whose taunts use only words the learner knows.
- Second Vietnamese review (of the redesigned lesson): hooks for hơn ("her" plus n, not "hun"), lăm ("lum"), mươi, bò in lesson 1 ("baw", since "Bo" gives bô or bồ), the đồng tip, the win line (trả lại tiền: trả tiền alone means to pay), mươi only from 20 up, a note that Hà Nội says bốn lăm or bốn nhăm when speaking fast, and hỏi described as a drop rather than a question rise.
- Rules added to PEDAGOGY.md from this round: judge difficulty on meaning; never teach one-mark pairs or near-synonyms together; one sound-alike plus one distinct image per hook; foe lines in known words; avoid hỏi against huyền with the current voice.

## 2026-11-20: Lesson 1, Phở and friends

- Data: none yet (first lesson). The learner said they are an upper beginner, know some words and tones, want Northern Vietnamese, and want a large vocabulary before sentences.
- Theme: food and drink. Concrete, useful in Hà Nội, and the words combine into real dishes (phở bò, cơm gà, bún cá), which gives combos and the noun-first rule for free.
- Twelve words using ngang, huyền, sắc and hỏi. Nặng appears only in the contrast ngọn and ngã not at all, so an early lesson should bring in both (numbers help: một, sáu and bảy, and a ngã word like mỗi or ngã itself).
- Contrasts chosen as real Hà Nội minimal pairs: phở/phố/phờ, cơm/cốm, bún/bùn, gà/ga, bò/bó/bỏ, cá/cà/cả, rau/râu, trà/trả, ngon/ngón/ngọn.
- Reviewed by a Northern Vietnamese accuracy subagent: fixed the intro count (ngon is not a food), the rau tip (short a, "zow" not "zao"), the nước hook (one syllable, ư to ơ), dropped cám as a contrast; added notes on chè for tea, quá before and after a word, and Cá ngon! as a full sentence.
